01

The story, plainly told

Most student ventures are a deck and an ambition. This one has invoices, named enterprise clients on three continents, a payroll, and a profit line. What follows is how it happened, in the order it happened.

At seventeen

Founded with no capital and no connections.

Shiftora was started by Shreshth Daga while he was seventeen and still in full-time education. No family money, no investor cheque, no borrowed network. The first clients were won the hard way, by finding a problem an enterprise was paying people to solve manually and then building the system that solved it better.

Months 1 to 5

Enterprise clients across three countries.

Not pilots and not proofs of concept. Production systems inside a US legal marketplace's Salesforce environment, a consumer visa platform launched in the UAE, and a demand-forecasting engine at a listed Indian manufacturer. Three countries, three regulatory environments, three different buyers.

Inside 5 months

Past $50,000 in revenue. Profitable throughout.

Revenue crossed $50,000 in under five months from a standing start, and the company has been profitable rather than burning toward a funding round. Every stage of growth has been paid for by delivered work rather than borrowed money.

Today

A payroll, not a side project.

Shiftora now has five people employed directly, working alongside an extended engineering bench of thirteen to eighteen across Dubai and Mumbai. Becoming an employer, rather than simply an earner, was the point from the beginning. The company is still bootstrapped, still profitable, and still run by a founder sitting exams.

Throughout

All of it while enrolled.

None of this is a gap-year story or an after-graduation story. It happened, and is still happening, during a degree. That simultaneity is the unusual part, and it is the part an institution can actually point to.

What the money was earned for

Each engagement below states the client or sector, what Shiftora built, and the measured result. None are pilots. They span industries and geographies, which is the point: the work is a portfolio, not a single lucky project.

Legal Tech

United States
LegalMatch
USA · attorney-client marketplace

LegalMatch is an established platform connecting consumers with vetted attorneys across the United States. A large sales floor was spreading effort evenly across inbound leads, so high-intent prospects and dead ends received the same follow-up. Shiftora built an AI sales intelligence layer inside the client’s existing Salesforce environment.

What we built
  • Predictive lead scoring: calibrated models ranking every inbound prospect by likelihood of revenue conversion, with each rank explainable to the rep.
  • Cadence optimisation: an outbound email and sequencing layer that adjusts timing to each prospect’s response history.
  • Decision support, not replacement: both systems augment the existing workflow of a 130-strong sales team rather than replace it.

Travel & Visa

United Arab Emirates
Musafir
UAE · longest partnership

Visa processing was manual and document-heavy, with errors surfacing only at submission. Shiftora designed and launched the replacement: a consumer visa platform covering discovery and eligibility through application, payment, and tracking, comparable in scope to Atlys.

What we built
  • Document intelligence: OCR with confidence-routed extraction; low-confidence fields route to a human, and nothing submits until it validates against country rules.
  • Visa workflow engine: a versioned rule engine per route and visa type, so past decisions stay reproducible and auditable.
  • Platform: multi-tenant backend, a .NET Minimal API core, with Next.js and React Native surfaces across web and app.

Manufacturing

India · listed company
Nitco Tiles
India · listed · building materials

Ordering ran on spreadsheets and instinct against long supplier lead times. Every forecast miss became dead stock or a stockout weeks later, and nobody could say which SKUs to trust. Shiftora built a demand-forecasting system with its accuracy proven under a published backtest.

What we built
  • Ingestion and decomposition: normalising 36 months of history across SKUs, channels, and regions, then separating trend, season, and promotion uplift before any model sees it.
  • Forecast engine: a gradient-boosted ensemble with a 12-month rolling horizon and lead-time-aware reorder signals per SKU.
  • Backtest, every cycle: rolling-origin cross-validation against a frozen holdout, re-run on every retrain, so a figure that stops passing stops being claimed.

04

Why this is a story an institution can tell

Universities invest heavily in showing prospective students what their people go on to build. This is one such story, and it has six properties that make it unusually useful.

Property 01 · timing

It is happening now, on campus.

This is a current, enrolled student, not an alumnus recalled a decade later. The company is live, the clients are current, and the story belongs to the institution while it is still unfolding.

Property 02 · credibility

It survives scrutiny.

Named enterprise clients, backtested numbers, booked revenue, and five people on payroll. Nothing here needs to be taken on faith. Every figure in this document is verifiable, which is exactly what makes a marketing claim safe to publish.

Property 03 · the origin

It started at seventeen, from nothing.

Founded at seventeen with no capital and no inherited network, and past $50,000 in revenue inside five months. The age and the absence of a safety net are what make the achievement legible to a prospective student and their parents alike.

Property 04 · fit

It is what an education is for.

Entrepreneurship, global enterprise clients, employment created, and measurable real-world outcomes are the qualities prospective students, parents, and rankings weigh most. This story evidences all four in a single student.

Property 05 · usefulness

It is ready to use.

A concrete, verifiable answer to the question every applicant asks: what do students here actually go on to do. It can anchor a prospectus page, an open-day talk, or a press feature without further manufacturing.

Property 06 · reach

It is not a local story.

Clients in the United States, the UAE, and India mean the story reads as credibly in an international recruitment market as it does at home. The same facts work in every geography an institution recruits from.

The context

Institutions spend heavily to manufacture stories like this. Here, one already exists, it is current, and it is verifiable. That the founder started at seventeen, took no outside money, crossed $50,000 in revenue inside five months, and is still enrolled is precisely what makes it worth telling: the achievement and the affiliation are happening at the same time, in the same place.
